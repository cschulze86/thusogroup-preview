Independent evaluation of the Aid Fund for Syria

Client
Adam Smith International (Fund Management Agent, Aid Fund for Syria)
Funding
UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office
Country
Syria
Years
2026
Support
Evaluation
Sectors
Humanitarian response; Localisation

The question

The Aid Fund for Syria is a multi-donor humanitarian pooled fund that grew from a cross-border fund for northwest Syria into a nationwide mechanism after the December 2024 transition. Most of its funding goes directly to Syrian national and local organisations. Its Steering Board, its Fund Management Agent and its donors needed to know whether the fund's strategy and operating model remain fit for purpose for 2025–2030, and what to adjust.

The work

  • Evaluated a portfolio of 26 grants and top-ups, worth about US$35 million, delivered by 20 implementing partners across ten governorates.
  • Designed a theory-based, mixed-methods evaluation that assessed the fund as a system: how it designs for quality, how it enforces that design, and whether it works in practice. Used contribution analysis, outcome harvesting and value-for-money analysis, structured against the OECD DAC criteria.
  • Reviewed 444 documents and held 63 primary sessions with the fund management team, donors, implementing partners, anchor organisations and the local actors they support, including 24 community consultations in six governorates.
  • Fielded two Arabic-speaking teams in the northwest, newly accessible areas and the northeast, with daily central review of recordings and transcripts.
  • Measured the fund's sustainability indicator grant by grant through structured verification calls, using a scoring rubric and a two-track method for closed and active grants that the fund can now apply across its portfolio.
  • Added, at the donor's request, an assurance review of partner capacity assessments across a sample of nine partners.

What came out of it

An inception report with seven data collection tools, an anonymised data package, and a final evaluation report with 21 sequenced recommendations and five strategic decisions framed for the Steering Board and donors.

Led by Charles E. Schulze and Matthew Goldie-Scot, with Alexandra Tigan as gender adviser, supported by a national field team.
