Strategic review of UNICEF's Standby Partnership arrangements

Client
UNICEF Office of Emergency Programmes (EMOPS)
Funding
UNICEF
Country
Global
Years
2024–2026
Support
Policy advisory; Systems design
Sectors
Humanitarian surge; Partnerships

The question

Standby partners are governments, non-governmental organisations and companies that recruit, pay and send experts to work with UNICEF in emergencies. A small desk in UNICEF's Office of Emergency Programmes manages the arrangements with them. EMOPS commissioned the review at a time of transition, with two aims: make Standby a flexible source of extra staff at short notice, and make UNICEF the agency partners most want to support. UNICEF's restructuring and the 2025 funding cuts made the question of what it wants from Standby next more pressing.

The work

  • Reviewed the mechanism end to end: governance, demand from offices, supply from partners, performance, coordination with UNICEF's other surge routes, and the functions of the desk itself.
  • Mapped UNICEF's rules on Standby across 241 policies, procedures and guidance documents, separating limits set at UN level from UNICEF's own choices and working conventions.
  • Extracted and validated the desk's request-level records from 2019 onward, and benchmarked performance against Standby Partnership Network data.
  • Interviewed 40 people across UNICEF headquarters, regional and country offices, Standby partners and the governments that fund them, the network and other UN agencies, alongside twelve working sessions with EMOPS and the desk.
  • Assessed the legal and financial framework: partner agreements, deployment rules, cost-sharing, and how partners' in-kind contribution is valued and shown in UNICEF's accounts.

What came out of it

A strategic review in three parts (the mechanism today, its legal and financial framework, and the way forward), consolidated into a single report with a three-to-five-year vision for Standby. Six recommendations, from the desk's resilience and partner agreements to demand generation, partner offers and the recognition of partners' in-kind contribution, are brought together in a proposed UNICEF policy on Standby. Each change is valued and costed, with a roadmap setting out who decides and where to start.

Led by Charles E. Schulze.
